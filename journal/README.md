# Learning journal

Record how understanding changes: the problem, the attempt, the evidence, and the next step.
Detailed concept notes can stay beside their exercises in `week/`; use this folder for
reflection across sessions and links back to the code.

## Add an entry

1. Copy [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) to `journal/YYYY/YYYY-MM-DD.md` using the actual session date. Create the year folder when needed.
2. Replace the prompts with a short account in your own words. Five to ten minutes is enough.
3. Link the relevant code or commit and record what you actually ran.
4. Note any references or assistance, and one concrete thing to rebuild independently.

For an entry inside `journal/2026/`, a link to the October 25 logbook would be
`[Logbook practice](../../week/04/1025/recall.py)`.

At the end of a week, copy [WEEKLY_TEMPLATE.md](WEEKLY_TEMPLATE.md) to
`journal/YYYY/YYYY-MM-DD-weekly.md`. Use the review date in the filename.

## Useful reflections

- What did I expect, and what happened instead?
- What was the underlying cause of the bug?
- What evidence supports the fix? Which cases remain unchecked?
- What can I now explain or rebuild without looking it up?

For interview preparation, pick a real entry and practice explaining the problem,
your decision, the result, and the remaining limitation in about a minute.
Keep entries factual: distinguish attempted, working with help, independently rebuilt,
and covered by automated tests. Templates are prompts, not completed learning records.

This journal is public. Keep employer data, credentials, and private personal details out of entries.
